What Is a Tax Lien?

A tax lien is a legal claim placed on a property when the owner fails to pay property taxes.

Every year, local governments rely on property taxes to fund schools, roads, emergency services, and public infrastructure. When those taxes are not paid, the government records a tax lien against the property.

Instead of waiting years to collect the money, many counties sell these tax liens to investors through public auctions.

The investor pays the unpaid taxes on behalf of the property owner and, in return, receives the right to collect the debt plus interest.

If the owner repays the taxes, the investor earns the agreed interest.
If the owner does not resolve the debt within the legal timeframe, additional rights may be granted depending on local laws.

How Has This Worked Traditionally?

For decades, access to tax lien auctions has been limited.

Participation often required:
• Large amounts of capital
• Knowledge of local regulations
• Access to county auctions
• Specialized legal and administrative experience

As a result, many opportunities were primarily available to institutional investors, funds, and experienced participants.

The Blockchain Opportunity

Projects such as LienFi seek to explore how blockchain technology can improve transparency, accessibility, and efficiency within this market.

The long-term vision is to help more people understand and potentially participate in opportunities connected to tax liens through modern digital infrastructure.

While the traditional tax lien system has existed for generations, blockchain introduces the possibility of making information, ownership records, and participation more transparent and accessible.
