What Is a Tax Lien?

A tax lien is a legal claim placed on a property when the owner fails to pay property taxes.

Every year, local governments rely on property taxes to fund schools, roads, emergency services, and public infrastructure. When those taxes are not paid, the government records a tax lien against the property.

Instead of waiting years to collect the money, many counties sell these tax liens to investors through public auctions.

The investor pays the unpaid taxes on behalf of the property owner and, in return, receives the right to collect the debt plus interest.

If the owner repays the taxes, the investor earns the agreed interest.
If the owner does not resolve the debt within the legal timeframe, additional rights may be granted depending on local laws.
